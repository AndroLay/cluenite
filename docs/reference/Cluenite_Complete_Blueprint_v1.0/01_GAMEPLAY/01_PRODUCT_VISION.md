# 01 — Product Vision, Emotion, Audience & Design Principles

## Core promise

Cluenite turns a passive chat into a **detective team** without making a streamer stop doing what they love. Play an intuitive 40–60-second oddity between matches, or host a 35-minute mystery night in which each viewer can become the person who notices the clue everyone missed. Two communities can work from separate workstations and meet at the same breakthrough.

## What it is NOT

- Not a traditional hidden-object game where streamer clicks everything while chat watches.
- Not a stream poll deciding whether something is objectively true.
- Not a Boolean-formula exam, though causal testing can be a hidden puzzle type.
- Not AI free-chat that fabricates facts and changes the suspect every turn.
- Not a large open-world, combat-heavy RPG, horror simulator, or sprite-animation production.
- Not a game that requires another Twitch account login or external page for ordinary viewers.

## Intended emotions (check in a playtest)

1. **Curiosity (first 5s):** Something clearly doesn't add up. One question is visible.
2. **Agency (next 10–30s):** Viewer notices a detail or suggests a specific, executable action.
3. **Social energy:** Streamer talks, laughs, defends a theory; chat can offer evidence instead of just popularity.
4. **Aha / reversal:** An observable contradiction makes a prior interpretation less plausible.
5. **Shared closure:** Reveal reconstructs the case with evidence and specific contributions.
6. **Desire for another case:** Fresh surface, fresh reasoning pattern, not merely different switch colors.

## Five product pillars

**Pillar A — Explainable mysteries.** Every significant assertion maps to a case-defined fact. A correct verdict requires sufficient proof, not guess frequency.

**Pillar B — Everyone can matter.** Chat users can discover, match, challenge, propose an interpretation or ask for help. Distinct ideas are counted rather than duplicate message spam; every meaningful contributor can receive acknowledgment.

**Pillar C — Stream-friendly friction.** 3 actions to first quick game after authorization: choose quick mode → select start → display OBS overlay. Overlay can dismiss at any time; match resumed beats puzzle deadline.

**Pillar D — Parallel then converge.** When two broadcasters collaborate, each crew has independently interesting work and share evidence at explicit convergence nodes. Neither is a permanent assistant to the other.

**Pillar E — Cozy-cinematic style.** 2D illustrated props, documents, gentle suspense, legible overlays, lively reaction animations, not tiny text or brutal subject matter.

## Audiences and job-to-be-done

| Audience | Before | Cluenite should deliver |
|---|---|---|
| Main-game streamer | Awkward queue / pause between matches | One-click short entertaining chat activity; minimal scene disruption |
| Just Chatting / variety streamer | Wants low-setup conversation material | Playful discussion around plausible mini-mysteries |
| Mystery-focused streamer | Wants stream-worthy event with coherent narrative | Multi-chapter case, meaningful deductions and satisfying reveal |
| Co-stream hosts | Want better collaboration than both doing same puzzle | Different workstations, evidence handoffs and joint payoff |
| Casual viewer (mobile) | Watches while multitasking | One visible question and a short command; optional participation |
| Power viewer | Wants deeper reasoning | Theory testing, proof links, optional deeper investigation |
| Quiet viewer | Doesn't type | A coherent mystery story, visual progress and recap |

## North-star qualitative outcome

A real streamer voluntarily asks for another case, and multiple actual viewers explain one piece of reasoning they contributed — with no prompt to pretend to be entertained.

## Validation signals (hypotheses, not benchmarks)

- First-action clarity: ≥80% of new viewers can identify an available action within 15 seconds in an observed test.
- At least one meaningful viewer-originated game-state transition per quick round.
- A median case should yield one visual discovery or hypothesis change every 3–7 minutes for longer play; test and revise.
- In co-op tests, both groups independently change state and neither waits >90 seconds during normal gameplay (target).
- Replay intent: independently ask whether they want another round; log actual choice, not just stated enthusiasm.

## Critical constraints

Deadline demands a small finished vertical slice. If tradeoffs arise, protect **real Twitch integration + readable overlay + one genuinely fun proof/reveal sequence** ahead of extra cases, live AI, large art library and advanced co-op.
